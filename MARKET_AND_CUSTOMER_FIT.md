# Market and spacecraft fit — evidence review, 9 October 2026

This is a decision aid for the **fixed Gen5 academic study** and a requirements input for **future Gen6 research**. It is not a total-addressable-market forecast, a customer endorsement, a priced business case or a compatibility certificate. No customer interview, supplier quotation, host agreement or flight payload has yet established demand for VOLLEY.

Launch counts in the Drive report are **market context, not a serviceable VOLLEY market**. Most spacecraft counted have incompatible size, orbit, owner, mission or propulsion needs. A SpaceX list price and an industry production rate cannot be multiplied into a VOLLEY sales forecast.

## Who could need an individually commanded release state?

The likely **economic buyer** is the party that commits to delivered orbital states across a manifest: a carrier or orbital-transport operator, rideshare integrator, or constellation operator buying an integrated delivery service. A satellite-bus manufacturer may specify interfaces and qualify payloads without buying the system. These roles remain hypotheses.

| Candidate job and spacecraft | Why a commanded release might matter | Current Gen5 disposition |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Replenishment batch:** repeated small EO, communications, environmental or sensing buses with several destinations from one host | May reduce per-spacecraft initial orbit-shaping burden if a carrier already makes common maneuvers | **Research case only.** Gen5’s modeled 12 × 3U mass is 10.547 kg/customer, outside the thesis’s ±15% parity band against the ~6 kg/customer spring comparator, and no sampled finite-burn case delivers all 12. No buyer has validated the requested states. |
| **Mixed rideshare:** two to four qualified payloads needing different release states | Host plus programmed release could reduce sequential host moves in a narrow mission | **Open.** Customer interfaces, flight rules, timeline and conventional host-burn comparator are unknown. |
| **Low-burden 3U spacecraft:** little or no propulsion for initial distribution, with a separate disposal/avoidance plan | A deployer might supply a useful initial orbit change without satellite propulsion hardware | **Conditional.** Current 3U model predicts 16.029 m/s but fails mass parity and has unresolved magnetic exposure and payload loads. Do not infer every 3U satellite is compatible. |
| **PocketQube-class batch:** many very small spacecraft | A shared mechanism could amortize fixed hardware across more payloads | **Future redesign case.** Gen5 volumetric and cell calculations suggest a lower mass/customer, but a qualified cassette, contacts, containment, release loads and customer mission do not exist. This is not a demonstrated Gen5 accommodation. |
| **1U, ThinSat, 6U/12U or magnetic-sensitive payloads** | Some may value orbital state control | **No current product claim.** The designed 1U cell case still misses the 2 kg/customer screen; ThinSat and 12U fail the studied cell cross-section; larger classes require new geometry; modeled magnetic fields challenge payload compatibility. |
| **Single spacecraft already near its required orbit; university safe-separation mission** | Usually needs safe release, not extra orbit shaping | **Poor fit.** A qualified conventional dispenser is the control case and should win unless a specific paid state requirement says otherwise. |

These are **mission classes**, not named sales prospects. The cited Planet and Spire platforms show repeat production; they are not claimed as Gen5 payloads, customers or partners. The [ANYA-class host study](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1cNc7fc26bCYYdZr_wGMC4o9pjV989rGZ/view) was reviewed as a historical public-data *Gen6 reference*, with assumed thrust, mass, minimum burn and restart limits. It is not a Gen5 provider interface or evidence of Thrustworks compatibility, so its numerical outcomes are excluded from this market fit.

## Corrected mission and economic test

Compare the **same** launch opportunity, host starting state, payload mass and interfaces, manifest, target states, deadline, disposal obligation and reliability boundary across: (A) qualified ordinary dispensers and actual separation impulse, (B) ordinary dispensers plus host maneuvers, (C) spacecraft propulsion, (D) an available orbital-transport service, and (E) host plus VOLLEY. Release timing alone from an unchanged host with zero relative impulse creates **no persistent co-orbital phase offset** at a common epoch; the historical 468-second/30-degree claim is withdrawn.

Let `F` be VOLLEY’s extra fixed installed and integration burden over the best conventional option; `Iᵢ` its extra per-payload burden; `Bᵢ` each customer’s avoided cost or paid delivery value; `H` extra host propellant, power, recovery time and operations; and `R` incremental expected loss, including common-path failures. A positive case requires **ΣBᵢ > F + ΣIᵢ + H + R**. None of these terms has a defensible quoted value yet. The formula is a decision boundary, not a price prediction. The mass comparison already fails at the current 3U configuration, so a commercial case must earn that burden with a measured mission benefit or use a different architecture.

The Drive report suggests **roughly 5–50 m/s** as a future business *hypothesis*. This is **not** an achieved Gen5 command envelope, a customer requirement or a validated Gen6 design point. Gen5’s modeled rated shot is 16.029 m/s; its controllable range, complete manifest and host integration remain open. The **1 km/s-class** Gen6 upper objective is research, with ideal stroke, recoil, energy and payload-load costs that make it a different regime.

## Evidence needed before making a market claim

For each prospective buyer, collect one actual or recent mission: spacecraft class and magnetic/structural constraints; count and orbit; desired per-payload state with tolerance; deadline; host restart/slew resources; approved deployment interface and safety rules; disposal method; current alternative and its quote; loss from the current method; integration approver and payer. Populate a matched mission sheet for 2, 4 and 12 spacecraft, including a case where ordinary separation wins. Record dates, currencies, payload/provider document revisions and uncertainty. A favorable interview is not a signed customer requirement.

**Current decision:** the market and prior-art evidence justify a targeted buyer/mission discovery exercise. It does not make Gen5 a viable 3U product or turn Gen6 research into a promised 1 km/s release system.
