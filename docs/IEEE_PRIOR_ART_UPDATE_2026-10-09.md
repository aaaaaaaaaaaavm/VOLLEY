# Prior-art and comparator update for the Gen5 paper

**Checked 9 October 2026.** This is a targeted addition to [RELATED_WORK.md](RELATED_WORK.md), not a systematic review. Search used Crossref title queries and OpenAlex DOI metadata; the Zhao 2022 paper was also read at the publisher's open full-text page, and the NASA 2026 technology review was read at NASA. Metadata-only leads below are **not** evidence for numerical performance claims. A product page or published paper informs a comparator; it cannot validate VOLLEY hardware.

## Direct research comparators

| [Zhao et al., *Remote Sensing* 14, 4205 (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs14174205), **publisher full text checked** | A stacked-CubeSat prototype uses electromagnetic transport and ejection and varies separation speed with drive current. ## Product and mission comparators

NASA's [2026 Small Spacecraft State of the Art: integration, launch, deployment and orbital transport](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/integration-launch-and-deployment/) identifies several rail and tab dispensers, separation systems and orbital transport vehicles. It also notes that the launch provider or integrator commonly selects the actual dispenser. The [EXOpod Nova user manual](https://www.exolaunch.com/documents/EXOpod_Nova_User_Manual_June_2024.pdf) is a useful primary interface and installed-burden reference. NASA's review describes D-Orbit ION as a last-mile transport option. These are **competent alternatives**, not evidence that a customer needs VOLLEY.

For an IEEE paper, compare one fixed mission and payload set across: (1) ordinary dispenser with its actual spring impulse; (2) dispenser plus host maneuvers; (3) onboard propulsion or differential drag where feasible; (4) an orbital transport vehicle; (5) VOLLEY plus its installed hardware, host power/attitude work and shared failure exposure. Show cases where the conventional option wins. Use provider documents only for the exact revisions and dimensions they state. A published price or flight count cannot stand in for VOLLEY's unquoted installed cost.

## What this changes in the paper

1. Describe Gen5's contribution as a **system-level computational evaluation** of a proposed higher-speed, moderate-acceleration, shared deployer with unmodified-payload intent and full installed-burden accounting. Do not claim first programmable electromagnetic CubeSat release, first stack feed, or a qualified 3U interface.
2. Replace the duplicate Zhao 2022 bibliography entry and distinguish its reported 3U speed from its 1U speed. Avoid describing Gen5's predicted speed as a demonstrated extension.
3. Add a position-dependent 3-D Halbach end-effect and segmented-handover check as a decisive missing validation. The new paper only motivates the check; it does not supply a Gen5 correction factor.
4. Before submission, obtain the four metadata-only full texts above, make a comparison table with payload mass, acceleration, speed, release error, technology, article/test maturity and installed mass, and mark each blank as **not reported**, not zero.
5. Keep the conclusion bounded by the failed Gen5 3U mass screen, failed CAD fit, historical model energy remainder now arithmetically reconciled under assumptions, unmatched mission comparison and absence of measured data. Those negative findings are research results; they do not make a flight product.
