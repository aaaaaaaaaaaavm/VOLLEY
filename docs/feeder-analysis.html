VOLLEY / Orbital analysis / Feeder review

Feeding a payload is a separate engineering problem.

FDR-08 · 19 October 2026. Seven Gen6 concepts share a 200 mm lateral transfer task for passive 1U/3U envelope surrogates. The receiver, gate, captive transfer and arrest requirements are evaluated separately from the launcher motor.

No feeder is selected or released for manufacture. Native CAD and a clear pose do not demonstrate handoff, safe power-off restraint, joint strength, complete installed hardware or reliability. Gen5 remains the evaluated configuration with its original failures.

Inspect the actual geometry

The package contains fourteen editable native assemblies and twenty-eight STEP states. Exact intersections are checked at nine transfer stations, one stowed pose and five gate heights per case. The rectangular payload sweep covers only straight translation against fixed feed-state geometry. Nonlinear indexing, handoff, return, loaded deformation and contact are outside that proof.

The capture-rack cantilever screen also fails adverse mean-arrest cases: its 1 mm tooth root reaches approximately 1,973 MPa nominal bending under the largest computed mean force. Actual peak force and a selected material allowable are unknown; this is a redesign finding, not a working positive lock.

CT-01: continuous prescribed-cycle follow-up

19 October 2026. A separate captive-transfer coupon checks receiver capture and fork withdrawal across a complete prescribed rectilinear sequence. The gate retracts down through a floor slot; this requires 120 mm gate travel and new under-deck accommodation. Receiver locks occupy separate motion bands.

Local source,23 native/STEP pairs including the separate contact coupon, reports and retained failures. False lock indication, a gate falling onto a straddling payload and missing actual sled custody remain physical counterexamples. No feeder is selected.
