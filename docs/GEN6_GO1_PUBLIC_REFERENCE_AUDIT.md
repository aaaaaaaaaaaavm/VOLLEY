# Gen6 Go 1: public reference audit

**Status:** public-source service study, 12 October 2026. These references bound the surrogate 2 × 1U + 2 × 3U case. None is a customer, selected spacecraft, host interface approval, or VOLLEY compatibility finding.

| Source and revision | Verified datum | Appropriate use | Limit |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| [Cal Poly CubeSat Design Specification, Rev. 14.1 (2022)](https://www.cubesat.org/s/CDS-REV14_1-2022-02-09.pdf) | Table 1 gives 1U ≤ 2 kg and 3U ≤ 6 kg; 1U is nominally 10 cm cubical. | Generic class envelope and a current mass ceiling for a **study**, subject to dispenser approval. | The CDS explicitly leaves actual allowable mass and fit to the dispenser. It does not establish a VOLLEY ICD. |
| [EPFL SwissCube project](https://space.epfl.ch/research/past-projects/swisscube-project/) | SwissCube is a 10 cm, less than 1 kg 1U spacecraft. | Named flown 1U form-factor example. | The P129 1.33 kg study payload is **not** SwissCube's actual mass or qualification. |
| [MinXSS mission paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.05354), Table 1 | MinXSS is a 3U spacecraft; its reported total is 3511.2 g. | Named 3U resource and geometry precedent; 4 kg in P129 is a rounded surrogate, not MinXSS's mass. | This older paper cites a 4 kg Cal Poly limit and 4.8 kg NanoRacks limit, reflecting its era; do not substitute those for CDS Rev. 14.1. |
| [NanoRacks CubeSat Deployer IDD, NR-NRCSD-S0003 Rev. -](https://s3vi.ndc.nasa.gov/ssri-kb/static/resources/Nanoracks-CubeSat-Deployer-NRCSD-IDD.pdf) | The IDD permits combinations of 1U–5U within a 6U volume; Table 4.1.2-1 gives 1U 2.4 kg and 3U 4.8 kg; §4.1.6 requires survival of 0.5–2.0 m/s deployment and up to 5°/s/axis tip-off. | Real mixed-size dispenser and mechanical-interface comparison; shows which explicit acceptance values an authority may impose. | This is an **ISS/NRCSD interface**, not an interface for the 500 km SSO host in P129. Its speed and mass limits cannot be claimed as universal or as approval of a 3–9 m/s VOLLEY release. |
| [D-Orbit ION launch/deployment service](https://dorbit.space/launch-deployment/) | The provider describes propulsion-enabled movement among orbital destinations and CubeSat deployment using its own or third-party dispensers. | Evidence that an orbital transport host and commanded deployment service category exists. | The page provides no usable mass, propulsion, trajectory, release, pricing or ICD numbers for P129. D-Orbit is **not** P129's 300 kg / 220 s host and has not endorsed this study. |

The CDS and NRCSD give different 1U/3U mass ceilings because they serve different purposes and revisions. Both are explicitly identified above; the governing limit for a mission would be the actual provider's signed interface document and payload approval. The MinXSS paper's historical CDS value is not a current limit.

The 500 km sun-synchronous, 300 kg dry host with 10 kg of propellant and 220 s specific impulse remains a **declared mathematical surrogate**. The two 1U payloads use 1.33 kg each and the two 3U payloads 4 kg each, so they lie within the CDS Rev. 14.1 class masses. No public source in this audit supports their magnetic tolerance, shock limit, allowed deployment speed, available host pointing, propulsion duty cycle, tracking accuracy, separation clearance or disposal plan. These must be obtained before a real integration claim.
