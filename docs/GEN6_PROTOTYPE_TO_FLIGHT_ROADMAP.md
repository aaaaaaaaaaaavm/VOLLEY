# From the Gen5 finding to a flight development decision

**Decision record, 10 October 2026.** Gen5 is the evaluated computational baseline, including failed fit, mass and mission screens. Gen6 is the proposed **first instrumented prototype programme**, conditional on a selected mission and architecture. Gen7–Gen9 below are development gates, not completed designs, schedules, speed promises or flight bookings. A product must sell a delivered spacecraft state with an accepted interface and operational risk, not merely an impressive exit velocity.

## The service to prove

For each satellite, specify a target position and velocity (or agreed orbital elements), covariance/tolerance, reference epoch, delivery deadline, allowed pointing and load, collision-avoidance volume, post-release disposal duty, and acceptance authority. The host must specify starting state, thrust/restart/attitude resources, power, mass/volume allocation and provider deployment rules. The buyer, payer and current delivery alternative must be named. A different target epoch or missing installed hardware makes a comparison unfair.

The first reference is openly surrogate: twelve 4 kg 3U spacecraft, a 450 km circular starting orbit, 300 kg base host, 10 kg starting fuel and an illustrative +16.029 m/s inertial tangential one-event target. It is **not** a customer requirement. The [matched reference](MATCHED_MISSION_REFERENCE.md) closes no twelve-shot option, and [P123](../validation/P123_gen5_one_event_mass_screen.md) finds a +52.717 kg Gen5 device-plus-fuel penalty in a resized one-event proxy. An external reviewer should therefore ask for a *specific paid delivery job* where controlled state, cadence or payload-side savings outweigh that burden. [P124](../validation/P124_precision_delivery_design_space.md) shows how to derive a release/load/accuracy requirement from such a job; it does not establish achieved precision.

| Gate | Required evidence | Stop or change direction if… |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **G0 — mission need** | One real or explicitly surrogate payload/host/interface set; targets and tolerances at common epochs; spring plus competent host, onboard propulsion/drag and transport alternatives; buyer and integration-authority interviews or sourced service terms | No material mission benefit remains after counting installed mass, propellant, power, integration and shared failure exposure. A high release speed by itself does not pass. |
| **G1 — architecture selection** | At least two complete Gen6 candidates; native editable CAD/STEP, feed, restraint, release, return and brake; installed BOM/mass/CG/inertia and margins; selected electrical topology; fault tree and provider envelope | Every candidate clashes, exceeds its allocated load/resource budget, or cannot plausibly meet the service tolerance. Preserve negative candidates and their model revisions. |
| **G2 — predictive design** | Independent 3-D position-dependent force check; selected winding, inverter, source and derating; coupled circuit/thermal/dynamics; contact/tip-off/host reaction; repeated brake cycles; magnetic/structural and tolerance corners; convergence and uncertainty | Performance exists only with ideal phase, omitted losses or unqualified payload loads; a fault has no safe state; independent methods disagree beyond declared bands. |
| **G3 — Gen6 instrumented first prototype** | A controlled build and calibration plan, then actual measured force, current, speed/vector, contact, brake temperature, host impulse and repeated cycles. Separate subscale coupons from a representative 4 kg system test. Compare measurements to preregistered model bands. | Data fail the bands, measured command repeatability misses the mission-derived budget, or the mechanism damages/contaminates the payload surrogate. Redesign before scaling. |
| **G4 — Gen7 engineering model** | Representative payload and host simulator; full commanded range, sequential feed/abort/reset, worst-case tolerances, life-cycle and fault injections; design review of release and containment | A twelve-release campaign cannot be completed safely with adequate margin, or installed service benefit is not credible. |
| **G5 — Gen8 qualification candidate** | Provider ICD and accepted test levels; environmental, EMC/magnetic, shock, vibration, thermal-vacuum, cleanliness, reliability and disposal evidence; independent safety and mission assurance review | Provider or payload authority rejects the interface or qualification margins. Computational agreement alone cannot pass this gate. |
| **G6 — Gen9 flight demonstration and service decision** | Licensed/approved mission, on-orbit tracking of delivered states, multi-release performance, anomaly handling and disposal; measured service versus contracted alternatives | Flight data do not support reliable delivered-state service or viable installed economics. Operational service requires a separate acceptance decision. |

## First prototype scope

Choose the first Gen6 test speed from the customer state and the **payload's own allowed acceleration**. P124 finds that a 1.3 m full-stroke, constant-acceleration 10 g idealization gives only 15.97 m/s; 50 m/s implies about 98 g; 1 km/s implies about 39,220 g and 2 MJ payload kinetic energy for 4 kg. Peak load and real motor requirements can be worse. Thus a lower-speed, measured command and release-vector demonstrator is the defensible first prototype for an ordinary 3U class. The 1 km/s-class line remains a separate payload/stroke architecture research stream and cannot be transferred into Gen6 acceptance criteria without a new mission and load case.

A low-cost coupon can measure field, force or contact laws and de-risk a subsystem. It cannot establish whole-device repeatability, 3U compatibility or flight readiness. Before procurement, release one configuration identifier linking CAD, netlist, solver cases, BOM, test procedure, calibration, model predictions, acceptance bands and stop rules. Record adverse measurements. Do not replace a failed threshold after seeing data.

## What must change in the public claim

The claim available now is a **falsifiable system-level computational study** with a negative Gen5 selection finding and a reasoned prototype path. “Precision delivery” is the intended service and a requirement to be measured. It is not yet an observed VOLLEY capability, product-market fit, provider compatibility, IEEE acceptance or flight plan. A stronger hardware case requires a mission that pays for commanded state and a selected design that survives G1–G3; the present work neither proves nor rules out that later case.
