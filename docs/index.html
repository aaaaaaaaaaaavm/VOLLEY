VOLLEY: an electromagnetic orbital CubeSat deployer

A magazine-fed deployer that fires twelve unmodified CubeSats off a spent upper stage, each at a velocity commanded for it — the unserved regime between spring deployers (~2 m/s) and propulsive transfer vehicles (hundreds of m/s).

The idea

CubeSats flown as rideshare secondaries inherit the primary customer's orbit. The spring that ejects them adds 1-2 m/s. That is a change in orbital energy — at 2.5 m/s it extends orbital lifetime by 8.2 % — but it is sized for separation, two orders of magnitude short of commanded orbit shaping, and the same value for every satellite in the manifest. Of more than 4,800 catalogued nanosatellites and CubeSats, on the order of 222 carry a propulsion system, so the rest stay where the manifest put them. That is a distribution problem, not a deployment problem — a constellation is only a constellation once its members are distributed.

VOLLEY replaces the spring with a magazine and a commanded shot. Twelve satellites, one at a time, each leaving at a velocity chosen for it. The satellite is never modified — no armature, no plating, no electrical interface.

The machine is half the idea. A launch vehicle's upper stage does its job in ten minutes and then becomes debris — deorbited, or left to decay. POEM has already flown the counter-example: a spent PS4 operated as a stabilised platform after its job is done. VOLLEY is what makes that reuse worth something. After the primary payload separates the stage becomes a last-mile delivery vehicle: it repositions between altitude shells on its own reaction control, fires satellites off at individually commanded velocities at each station, and makes a final burn to reenter.

That direction was set in 2023, by ADR-002 — "learning of ISRO's POEM, a spent PSLV fourth stage operated as a stabilised platform, reframed the problem". A free-flyer must carry attitude control, power and recoil management, "which is most of a spacecraft"; a spent stage already has all three. Every architecture decision since has moved the design from riding a stage to being one.

With the boundary attached, because a delivery claim is easy to overstate. An altitude shell change costs 27.8 m/s; along-track distribution is what the deployer itself does; nodal separation develops free from differential J2, computed at 367° over 90 days in GMAT. Plane change is excluded — 133 m/s per degree, eight times the whole shot — and no claim here implies VOLLEY or its host selects an orbit plane.

Two machines, and every number belongs to one of them

Gen5 is the frozen baseline that every headline number is computed against. Gen6 is the current design target, and it is a different machine.

Gas supplies the energy; the motor supplies the control. That division is deliberate. A gas store charges slowly from solar and releases fast — an excellent energy store and a terrible servo. A linear machine is the reverse.

The motor is not decoration at half a percent of the stroke. Gen6's shot is a single open-loop expansion dispersing at 3.980 % (3σ), of which 98.7 % is a seal friction nobody has measured — and A44 found no instrumentation route out, because a fivefold better pressure transducer moves it 0.008 %. The stator is what recovers the commanded velocity the product is sold on. Take it out and this is a gas gun with a scatter it cannot correct.

Why the store is gas

A39 ran the trade against a 12.55 kg budget for store plus mechanism, at 32.7 m/s. Gas won by a factor of four, and the reason is not energy density.

Keep the motor (control)23.76 kg—23.76 kgevery velocity
The spring's problem was never storing the energy. It was having to be cocked twelve times. A spring stores 7.13 kg of energy and then needs 4.28 kg of mechanism to re-arm it. Gas separates the store from the actuator, so re-arming is a valve and one bottle runs the whole manifest.

Every alternative was screened by a run on the record. A supercapacitor bank and the full motor — the bank cannot source the shot on cells anyone sells: the shot needs ≤ 68 mΩ and a real 32 × 190 F string is 116–185 mΩ (P26). A flywheel — the one live alternative, clearing the ceiling at 35 mΩ but at mass parity, not a saving (P45). A lead screw — DN limit exceeded 8×, whirling 36×. Rack and pinion — contact drive at full speed in vacuum.

And gas has already failed once here. A39 chose it while assuming a regulator it never named, and A40 killed that implementation at 14.16 m/s against a 30 m/s band. A41's pre-charged chamber is the repair: charge slowly, fire as a closed expansion, no flow-rate problem by construction.

A shot, end to end

Gen5. Feed, retention gate, 1.3 m of acceleration at 10.07 g, coast and trim, release at 1500 mm, regenerative braking over the next 240 mm, eddy brake, sled recovered for the next shot. Regeneration returns 291 J of the sled's 1268 J to the bank and the brake absorbs the remaining 935 J, so efficiency is quoted electrical-to-payload net of that credit.

Gen6. A 3.46 L bottle at 200 bar charges a 2 L chamber to 22.73 bar during the twenty minutes between shots — 0.26 W average, 36 W peak. The chamber opens as a closed adiabatic expansion and a carriage pushes the satellite down 8.0 m of tube. The 144 mm stator, energised after the gas has finished, corrects the velocity to ±1.1543 m/s. The carriage is not recovered.

That stator is suspended, and the seal may delete it — ADR-036, 20 August 2026. The dispersion it exists to correct is computed at A41's friction allowance, a ceiling rather than a measurement. A61 asked instead what the loosest seal is that the design can survive and found the binding requirement is thermal: a 2 g seal must stay within 50 K of its own friction heating, which needs 17.8 N. The trim stage stops earning its mass at 22.3 N — so any seal that survives its own heat also makes the stator unnecessary. Work on it stops until P67 measures the friction. And the honest limit: at that seal the dispersion is 0.9051 % against a declared band of 0.5 %. Unnecessary and sufficient are different words.

A cold-gas module beats both on mass at 3U by 7.5×, and a ~1.8 kg staged spring reaches the same velocity inside the g-cap. What VOLLEY sells is a fleet distributed on a schedule, not a better push.

The reliability row is the one Gen6 improves least. A47 found that deleting six subsystems removed exactly one shared failure, and added the host stage's keep-alive agreement, which no launch provider has given. A per-cell backup ejector is worth six times the whole architecture change — and A53 found it cannot clear a sealed tube, missing by 40× at 2.18 m and 148× at 8 m (P81).

Control stability, and a gain that was never designed

The dispersion figure above was produced by a loop nobody had drawn a Bode plot of. When one was drawn, the published gain turned out to be linearly unstable.

CFD: what air costs a ground test

The machine flies in vacuum. The full-scale demonstration fires a mass simulator down a 1.5 m track in a room, and nothing said what the air was worth. Steady RANS on the script-built Gen5 CAD — meshed from the STL the repository ships, not an idealised box.

GMAT cross-check, the first external result, and it found something

GMAT R2022a, run headless with MSISE90 / 20x20 gravity / RK89, propagated to the 120 km floor at three solar-activity levels. The x1.80 lifetime multiplier of the day reproduces at mean and high activity. At low activity it does not, and the invariance the paper nominated as its defensible result is falsified. These runs were propagated at 20.37 m/s, before the CAD-derived sled mass moved the rated point to 16.029 m/s, the falsification survives, since a uniform density scale cannot move a ratio at any Δv, but the absolute numbers are historical (P19).

High, F10.7 250x1.7302
Mean, F10.7 150x1.7750
Low, F10.7 70x2.0739
astro.py claimx1.80

Spread 18.48 % against a ≤5 % band declared before the run, A5's verdict is FAIL. The mechanism was tested, not guessed: astro.py models solar activity as a uniform multiplicative scale on density, and sweeping that scale over a 40x range moves the multiplier only 1.7992 to 1.7968. A uniform density factor divides both lifetimes by the same number, so the ratio is invariant by construction of the model. MSIS instead changes the shape of the density, altitude profile with F10.7, the boosted orbit's apogee sits ~37 km higher, and the ratio then moves. Written up as P16.

Separately, over a bounded 30-day window the fitted decay rates are −0.1618 km/day (GMAT) against −0.1216 km/day (astro.py), GMAT decays 1.33x faster. Reported SMA is osculating and its short-period variation runs 12.2 km peak to peak, several times the decay across the window, so the comparison is a least-squares rate over 31 daily samples rather than a difference of endpoints.

Where the energy goes

Gen5. 2782 J leaves the capacitor bank per shot, 291 J comes back through the regenerative section, and 514 J of the net 2735 J reaches the payload — the 18.5 %. Source sizing.py energy_closure; 100.0 % accounted. This chart said "no regeneration credit" and put the sled's whole energy in the brake until 2026-07-31. The 2025 decision behind that argued the motor cannot arrest the sled, which it cannot — the brake still takes 935 J of every shot. It never argued that none of the energy could be recovered, and A11 found 23 % of it available. The ESR bar has its own history: no script modelled the bank's series resistance until 2026-07-30, so the loss was real in hardware and absent from the accounting until a circuit simulation found it.

Gen6 has no chart of this kind and the absence is the finding. Its energy arrives as a ground-filled bottle, so there is no electrical-to-payload ratio to draw. The 25–131 W this project quoted until 2026-08-16 was a spring-winding figure for a machine that has no spring (P80).

What has actually been verified

Sixty-five run sheets, A1 through A65, each against an acceptance band declared in writing before the script existed. Three failed outright, several missed individual bands, and three times a declared band caught a bug in the analysis rather than in the design — which is exactly what declaring the band first is for. Nothing has been validated by hardware; every row is one model against another model.

The failures are the reason to trust the rest. The paper once carried four numbers its own scripts did not reproduce, all found by rebuilding the analysis from scratch. An independent propagator then falsified a claim in the paper's own abstract, and that is recorded as P16 rather than quietly dropped. Every defect is numbered and published, including the ones that damage the project's own claims — there are 130 of them, 53 live.

Reproducing it

Five scripts, two dependencies, roughly twelve minutes end to end. Results land in analysis/results/*.json. The CAD rebuilds byte-identically from cad/parameters.json with python3 cad/build_gen5.py and build_gen6.py.
