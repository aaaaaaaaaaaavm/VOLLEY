VOLLEY investigates provider-hosted control of individual payload departure conditions: relative velocity, direction and timing supplied at release, before a spacecraft has to carry mobility of its own.

01 · the job

A deployer is already changing the orbit. The question is whether that change can be useful.

The host owns coarse orbital state and resources. The deployment system owns the relative release condition. The spacecraft owns everything after contact ends. VOLLEY is the trade between those three.

host

Place and recover

Navigation, attitude authority, permitted manoeuvres, recoil recovery and campaign reserves stay in host accounting.

release

Control the departure

Relative Δv, direction, timing, retention, tip-off and repeatability are the deployment-system job.

spacecraft

Then it is on its own

No continuing stationkeeping, collision avoidance or formation control is implied after separation.

flight context

The host-side mission already exists in pieces. VOLLEY asks whether the release can do more.

These are real spacecraft and upper-stage operations, not renders of VOLLEY. They establish useful context for retained orbital platforms and multi-satellite separation campaigns; they do not demonstrate provider compatibility with this project.

ISRO · POEM-4

A spent upper stage operated as an orbital platform

The useful precedent is the host: power, navigation, attitude and post-primary-mission operations can remain with a controlled stage.

ISRO · LVM3-M2

A real 36-satellite separation campaign

Sequencing, reorientation and safe separation are already launch-vehicle mission problems. VOLLEY asks what changes if departure conditions become deliberately programmable.

02 · the machine

Five years of deleting assumptions.

The mission idea survived. The mechanism did not. The current redesign is deliberately not rendered until architecture selection is earned.

2021

Coilgun on a free-flyer

The project began by naming the distribution problem. In 2023 the host changed to a spent upper stage; by mid-2025 the coilgun gave way to a linear synchronous motor because commanded velocity, not simply ejection, was the product.

2023 ADR-002

POEM reframed the mission around a retained, controlled upper stage rather than a dedicated free-flyer.

Gen4

Last hand-modelled generation

Detailed Fusion geometry, but no committed STEP export and known disagreement with analysis stations.

9 Fusion documents

More visual detail did not mean more reproducibility. Gen4 is useful history, not a current numerical authority.

Gen5 · frozen

Electromagnetic baseline

Script-built geometry and the manuscript comparator. It is reproducible, heavy, and intentionally frozen with its failures.
