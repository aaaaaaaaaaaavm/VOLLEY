# VOLLEY next-generation status

**Gen6 is in development. No architecture, hardware configuration or performance envelope is selected or validated.** Nothing has been built, fired, measured, qualified or flown.

The design objective is a reusable launch path with a controlled loader that presents CubeSats one after another. Each release should have a commanded relative speed within an eventual, evidence-based operating envelope. Ordinary, propulsionless payloads without a special powered interface are the intended case; provider and payload compatibility remain open.

The earlier bank of separate motor-charged spring cells is withdrawn as a current architecture. Its slow charger stores energy mechanically; the spring, not the motor's electromagnetic field, accelerates the payload. After a cell fires, its empty structure remains attached. The claimed jam-isolation benefit had no measured failure probabilities or matched installed-mass/mission comparison. The older long gas guide is also unselected. Both remain historical engineering studies, with model results and failures retained as such.

The S4 two-payload ideal mission screen produced a best sampled point near 4.57 m/s under its chosen host and timing assumptions. That number cannot define the product speed ceiling. A subsequent twelve-payload finite-burn screen failed complete delivery in six tested cases, best 5/12; it is a bounded model failure, not a proof that all missions fail. Neither study demonstrates a full-manifest benefit over conventional deployment.

The requested examples at 1–2, 30–40 and around 100 m/s are **investigation points**, not a demonstrated range. For a 4 kg payload, ideal energy and acceleration distance increase sharply with speed. At 100 m/s, energy is 20 kJ and constant-acceleration stroke is about 51 m at 10 g or 34 m at 15 g. Those acceleration levels are illustrations, not universal CubeSat limits. Payload-specific structural, contact, internal-component and functional qualification is needed.

The next decision requires a matched comparison of shared electromagnetic and other shared drive/feed concepts, banked paths, historical gas/spring options and a competent ordinary dispenser. Count complete installed mass and volume after each shot, common and local failures, host power and pointing, clearing time, payload interfaces and the entire mission manifest. A selected physical mechanism and calibrated multi-shot measurements are needed before claiming release accuracy, repeatability or velocity authority.
