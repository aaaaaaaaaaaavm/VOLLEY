# Higher-speed design constraints

Current ACR1 computations support command points **1, 2 and 3 m/s**. A separate study kept the same geometry, source, controller and acceptance gates while requesting 10, 15, 30, 50 and 100 m/s. None of its 22 cases completed the required release and arrest conditions; four timestep refinements retained that outcome. [All cases](https://github.com/aaaaaaaaaaaavm/VOLLEY-lab/blob/a20118307f168a7e639fd7ffa0de477880a5054b/experiments/higher-speed-envelope/MODEL_CASES.csv) and [source/results](https://github.com/aaaaaaaaaaaavm/VOLLEY-lab/blob/a20118307f168a7e639fd7ffa0de477880a5054b/experiments/higher-speed-envelope/HIGH_TARGET_RESULTS.json) preserve the actual findings.

The accelerated assembly is 17.127806 kg, including the 13.127806 kg retained sled/pallet and 4 kg payload. The current 210 mm brake map could supply at most 141.76 J even under an ideal instantaneous constant 600 A current. A 10 m/s release would require 644.09 J of relative sled-arrest work before losses. This identifies a new arrest architecture, source/controller capacity and coupled design as prerequisites for a 10–15 m/s development target. It establishes no new operating rating. [Constraint figure](https://github.com/aaaaaaaaaaaavm/VOLLEY-lab/blob/a20118307f168a7e639fd7ffa0de477880a5054b/experiments/higher-speed-envelope/HIGH_VELOCITY_SCREEN.png) · [Detailed bounds](https://github.com/aaaaaaaaaaaavm/VOLLEY-lab/blob/a20118307f168a7e639fd7ffa0de477880a5054b/experiments/higher-speed-envelope/CONSTRAINTS.csv).

Published laboratory magnetic-field, force and low-speed release measurements provide useful benchmarks at their own geometry and conditions. They can support applicable equations after a documented reconstruction; they do not physically validate VOLLEY or its higher-speed scenarios. [Experimental source cards](https://github.com/aaaaaaaaaaaavm/VOLLEY-lab/blob/a20118307f168a7e639fd7ffa0de477880a5054b/experiments/higher-speed-envelope/EXPERIMENTAL_BENCHMARK_CARDS.json).

A separate Gen6 trade considers an adapter that remains attached to the departing payload. It removes successful-departure sled arrest but changes interfaces, recoil and twelve-adapter inventory. The illustrative 0.25/0.5/1 kg shoes cannot retain the current 3.6288 kg magnet set, so their mass-only speed ratios require a redesigned electromagnetic geometry. ## Sampled operating-point extension

A subsequent [current-model scan](https://github.com/aaaaaaaaaaaavm/VOLLEY-lab/tree/245b2bcb523a3b2ce9f160c4350bb9f804e2905b/experiments/speed-envelope-extension) contains 106 records, including 88 new computations and 17 timestep refinements. Common sampled upper commands pass the model's release/arrest gates at **4.60 m/s nominal and 3.70 m/s adverse**, at each of the three tested inductances. These are discrete model results, with a 0.05 m/s upper bracket; they do not establish a continuous operating interval or a new hardware rating. Full exit clearance and powered reset are outside this shot model. Gen5's published operating cases remain 1, 2 and 3 m/s.

The [published force benchmark](https://github.com/aaaaaaaaaaaavm/VOLLEY-lab/tree/36424411119917cdc4b10b3e080e9f3c2ff6a56b/experiments/published-force-benchmark) reconstructs five external experimental points with explicit calibration and holdout roles. Its fitted coefficient is specific to that experiment and is not substituted into VOLLEY's finite motor.

The [lunar architecture screen](https://github.com/aaaaaaaaaaaavm/VOLLEY-lab/tree/36424411119917cdc4b10b3e080e9f3c2ff6a56b/experiments/lunar-delivery-design-space) separates Earth departure, finite-host recoil and lunar capture. Its ideal coplanar 500 km Earth-orbit to 100 km lunar-orbit reference requires approximately 3.058 km/s departure and 0.820 km/s capture. These are analytical resource bounds with an independently integrated Earth leg, not an optimized lunar encounter or a claimed launcher capability.

## New 10 m/s development configurations

The separately designed [R10/F10 study](https://github.com/aaaaaaaaaaaavm/VOLLEY-lab/tree/21c46b09b58176488403808f5f6053681751e4bf/experiments/ten-metre-per-second-design) now includes new finite windings, resistance/inductance calculations, mass/source ledgers and twelve actual circuit/motion cases. **R10 supports its declared nominal and adverse 10 m/s cases after timestep and field refinement.** It uses 2.5 m powered travel, 1.3 m active arrest, a 4.75 m rail and a 192-cell pulse store; its modeled/allocated device dry mass is about 179.5 kg. This is a new research configuration, not the current Gen5 mechanism or a manufacturing/flight release.

The attached-shoe alternative reduces modeled source energy but carries twelve modified-payload adapters and misses its adverse speed tolerance. The study therefore identifies R10 as the stronger present computational development candidate. Actual source switching, complete feeder/return operation, structural and payload interfaces, and hardware correlation retain their separate development gates.

A [computed five-day Earth–Moon transfer](https://github.com/aaaaaaaaaaaavm/VOLLEY-lab/blob/21c46b09b58176488403808f5f6053681751e4bf/experiments/lunar-delivery-design-space/THREE_BODY_RESULTS.json) supplements the analytical lunar screen. In an ideal coplanar circular-Moon model it uses approximately 3057.50 m/s Earth departure and an explicitly applied 813.84 m/s capture impulse, followed by a 24-hour coast near 100 km lunar altitude. The case does not claim a real-date SSO trajectory, long-term lifetime, passive capture or an electromagnetic launcher capable of kilometre-per-second injection.
