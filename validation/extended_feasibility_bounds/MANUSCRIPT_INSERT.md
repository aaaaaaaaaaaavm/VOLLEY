The lunar extension remains an ideal requirements screen. A connected impulsive model gives Earth departure 3.106427 km/s and direct capture into a 100 km circular lunar orbit 821.239408 m/s. Capture into a 100 × 10,000 km ellipse reduces initial capture to 306.643674 m/s, but later same-perilune circularization restores the same total impulse. Useful lifetime, navigation covariance, finite carrier burns and independent disposal remain unresolved. A separate 1 km/s release compatibility check for a 4 kg payload and 300 kg host requires at least 1.973684 MJ added relative kinetic energy and 2.012598 km constant-force stroke at the 25 g study ceiling. A compact low-acceleration mechanism therefore cannot meet that upper speed target under the declared combination of requirements. These are conditional analytical bounds, not selected hardware, provider performance or flight validation.
